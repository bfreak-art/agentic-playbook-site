What's an AI agent?

You give it access to your inbox and a standing instruction. Then it gets on with it — every morning, without being asked.

7:00 — sorted 23 new emails
7:02 — drafted 14 routine replies for your review
7:03 — flagged 3 that need you

Your agent, working while you have breakfast.

You ask a chatbot to draft a reply to a customer. It helps — but only when you ask, and only with what you paste in.

“Draft a reply to this email from Sarah…”
“Hi Sarah, thanks for getting in touch…”

Tomorrow you'll do it all again.

The problem

Most founders learn agents the hard way.

Agents can take real work off a small team — research, admin, follow-ups. But most founders build them alone, by trial and error. Then the agents break in ways nobody notices.

72.5%

learned to use agents by trial and error (Stadnik, 2026)

15%

had any structured training (Stadnik, 2026)

0 of 12

teams interviewed had alerts for when an agent quietly stops working (Stadnik, 2026)

The good news: most failures came from how the agent was set up, not the AI itself — unclear instructions, trusting output without checking, too much freedom too soon. These are skills, and skills can be taught.

My approach

Earn trust in an agent one step at a time.

I didn't invent these four steps. They're the pattern I found in the teams whose agents held up. Tap each step to see it in action.

Step 1 of 4

Do it by hand, with AI

Run the task yourself with an AI assistant until the result is right. No automation yet — just learning what “good” looks like.

For example

You paste this week's customer emails into ChatGPT and refine the replies until you'd happily send them.

Tools change every few months. The method doesn't — so the method is what you keep.
